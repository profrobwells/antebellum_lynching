Full Context: Western Virginia, states that a pilgrim from Yankee land, traveling through the
country, was suspected of being an Abolition emissary by the owner of a
plantation of negroes, and taken up by him and hung up several times by the

punishment, he was let go. The person who did this job is described by the
paper above alluded to as 'one of the most influential and worthy citizens' of
those parts. We regret to hear of such violence. We think that influential and
worthy citizens should leave such business to ruffians and worthless citizens,
as being more becoming to such hands. We should suppose that the laws of
Virginia were sufficiently expansive to meet the necessity of protecting the
rights of property in all cases that might arise, whether from Abolition
emissaries or any other source. Whether this be so or not it is policy fraught
with great danger to the Union, to take up every Northern man who may be simply
suspected of being an Abolitionist and abusing him with such outrage as was
committed in the case above alluded to. The most innocent of traveler is liable
to be suspected. No man can be supposed to have the ability to make his true
character appear in every instance, to the jaundiced gaze of Southern fanatic
whose daily fancies and nightly dreams are haunted by horrent ideas of servile
insurrections, bloody murder and black Abolition conspiracies. Furthermore,
even if it were known that the Northern peddler or school teacher was a
thoroughbred Abolitionist, does the Constitution of our common country permit
him to be treated as an outlaw, and to be subject to lawless violence? Let him
be tried by the laws of the land, and, whatever his doom may be, the North has
no right to complain. But the Northern bosom must be callous indeed, if it
would remain unmoved at the assumption of authority by every individual citizen
of the South to pounce upon any Northern man they chose who came into the
country, and hold him to personal responsibility for his supposed political
opinions. If these things are to be allowed in the South, how long will it be
before the North retaliates? Not long. We shall hear soon if such violence
continues, of Southern travelers through the North being suspected of design to
spirit away the free colored men of the North, and the next thing will be the
stringing up and lynching of Southern emissaries and suspected 'nigger
drivers'. The rule is as fair for one side as the other, and it will be acted
up to. With such reciprocity of violence between citizens of the North and the
South, how long time would elapse before the country would be involved in civil
war? The true rule of action is to respect the laws of either section of the
Union; to treat Northern men with the same regard in the South as Southern men
have been treated in the North; to allow every Northern man in the South the
same freedom of expression within the limits of the Constitution and law, that
is allowed Southern men in the North, and to punish all offenses against the
State, whether of the Northern or Southern section, by the laws of that State.
John Brown has doubtless been hung. His doom was just. He had forfeited his
life by open violation of the laws of Virginia. He was fairly tried and legally
convicted, and few there are who would dispute the right of Virginia to execute
him. But, if he had been apprehended by lawless mob, convicted by Lynch
tribunal and put to death by like authority, you would have seen a storm of
wrath, dark and portentous, gathering upon the brow of the North, and it would
have burst, in due time, with fearful effect. She would have been justly
incensed. In the same degree, then, may she feel outraged when one of the
obscurest of her citizens is taken up and physically abused, under the general
sanction of the public opinion of the South. The times are far too dangerous to
admit of alterations from the due course of law and justice. It will not do to
further embitter the deep prejudices of thousands. For every Southern man slain
in Kansas hundred fanatics have arisen in the South. For every Northern man
tarred and feathered by Southern planters thousand fanatics have arisen in the
North. And so the feeling grows and expands. By all that is holy in our Union,
let violence cease.
