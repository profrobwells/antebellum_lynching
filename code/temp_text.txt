- We regret to learn that the indefatigable Mr. Niblo has
met with severe loss. A fire broke out yesterday, at 12
o'clock, in the building at the north end of the main
house, used for exhibition, and in part by Mr. Hall, for
preparing fireworks. The fire was caused by the bursting of
a bottle of composition called crimson fire from
spontaneous combustion, a large quantity of fireworks
immediately exploded, and Mr. Hall and his men narrowly
escaped. The explosion destroyed the house and everything
it contained. The flames soon extended to the main
building; we regret to state the damage was very great;
much valuable furniture was handed out of the windows and
saved, but a great portion of it was destroyed together
with the whole interior of this great house. The
magnificent painting of the departure of the Israelites
from Egypt which belonged to a Mr. Wright was destroyed.
One of the colored waiters being cut off from any retreat,
was burnt to death in one of the upper stories.
Melancholy.-The wife of Mr. Samuel Story, of this city,
hurned to death Tuesday evening last by the taking fire of
her clothes. She was alone in her room at the time of the
occurrence, and expired soon after assistance reached her.
It is supposed that while attempting to close a window, the
flame of a lamp communicated to her dress. Norwich

Bodies found. The British Consul at Tangiers has written to
Lloyds that 18 dead bodies of Christians had been found on
the beach. Some of the bodies were found handcuffed and all
of them had been stabbed in various places. All the bodies
appeared to have had manacles on them; but the natives had
taken them off for the most for the value of the iron. A
gentleman and his lady made a move to the West. After a
short sojourn they made another move, then also, and after
that the third. Still onward, it was during the third move
that the narrator happened to fall in with this family. He
referred to the east, the home they had left, and inquired
if it would be agreeable to revisit them. The lady with all
the attachment of a woman's heart, to the husband of her
love, resolved, however, to continue in his company, lead
where he might. But shaking her head, at the prospect of
another move she said, amidst all this I have one
consolation, and that is derived from the circumstance that
the earth is round, and by keeping on we shall get to

living.-A shrewd man will turn everything to good account.
Here is a case, for instance. A farmer in this town had a
fine field of rutabaga, which was overrun with
grasshoppers, eating off the leaves and threatening the
entire destruction of the crop. He procured a drove of
turkeys and turned them into the lot and they made
mincemeat of the grasshoppers. The rutabagas were saved and
